The musician behind Balkazar

Belgrade roots.
Taipei rhythm.

From punk clubs in Serbia to Balkan-fueled stages in Taiwan. A guitar, a voice, and a taste for music that refuses to stay in one box.

Marko Djurasic · Marko Fuzzy Balkañero
Guitarist · Vocalist · Founder of Balkazar

Follow Marko on Instagram ↗ His story ↓

Marko Djurasic, also known onstage as Marko Fuzzy Balkañero, is a Serbian guitarist, singer and bandleader based in Taiwan. He is the founder and frontman of Balkazar, a Taipei-based band bringing Balkan melodies together with ska, reggae, rock, funk and the energy of an audience ready to dance.

Guitars first. Belgrade.

Marko's musical path began in the independent rock and punk scene of Belgrade, Serbia. Before Taiwan, he played with local acts including Potkrovlje 25 and Prljava Familija—the kind of bands where a song comes alive onstage, not on paper.

A new sound in Taiwan.

In 2012 he co-founded La Cumbia Balkanska with musicians from Colombia and France. The project mixed the melodies and rhythms of the Balkans with Colombian cumbia, building a lively international lineup in Taipei. The group also took its music to Japan in 2013.

As the musicians followed different paths, the original project branched: a cumbia-oriented direction continued as La Cumbia del Sol, while Marko pursued the Balkan-focused sound that grew into Balkazar. It was a musical fork in the road, not simply a change of band name.

Building Balkazar.

By 2017, Balkazar was appearing at Urban Nomad Opening Freakout and on Taipei's club stages. Since then, the band's performances have ranged from live houses to festivals and public cultural celebrations, including Taiwan Ska Fest and Celebration Canada.

One memorable collaboration came in 2019, when Balkazar joined Basque musician Fermín Muguruza for a ska night connected to the Urban Nomad film festival in Taipei. Marko continues to shape Balkazar as a live band built on strong playing, improvisation, and a connection with the crowd.

Still a punk guitarist.

His other musical life runs on distortion. In Taiwan he has also fronted the garage/noise-punk project Earsore and played with BRIJ. Today he plays guitar in Fuzzy Wall, a Taipei garage-punk and alternative rock band. Those two sides—Balkan rhythms and gritty rock guitars—are part of the same musician.

Sharing the music.

Marko also introduces audiences to Balkan music through workshops and live demonstrations. These have included sessions at Vincent's Accordion Studio and the 2020 Balkan in Your Area workshop, where musicians explored distinctive Balkan rhythms, cultural influences and improvisation together.

Interested in a Balkan music workshop?

Marko can help put together an accessible, interactive introduction to Balkan music for schools, culture programs, music communities and festivals.

However the lineup or setting changes, the point is the same: bring good musicians together, play something worth feeling, and let the audience join in.
