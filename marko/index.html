Marko Djurasic is a Serbian guitarist and singer based in Taipei. He sings and plays electric guitar in Balkazar, which he helped start, and plays guitar in the garage-punk band Fuzzy Wall. Before that came rock gigs in Belgrade, a band mixing cumbia with Balkan tunes, and years of concerts, festivals and club nights around Taiwan. He sometimes performs as Marko Fuzzy Balkañero.

It started in Belgrade.

Before moving to Taiwan in 2010, Marko played lead guitar with Potkrovlje 25 and also played with Prljava Familija. Potkrovlje 25 played original rock material and took part in the Serbian GBOB Challenge qualifying round in 2008. A recording of the band's song Hej ljubavi survives in his archive. Those years meant plenty of rehearsals, club shows and figuring out songs with other musicians.

Then came Taipei. And cumbia.

In 2012, Marko and musicians from Colombia and France started La Cumbia Balkanska. They put Balkan melodies together with Colombian cumbia and played in Taiwan and Japan. There's an early 2012 concert video further down the page. Later the musicians went in different directions: La Cumbia del Sol on one side, while Marko and other musicians formed Balkazar on the other.

From small clubs to festival stages.

Balkazar has taken Marko from Taipei's bars to Urban Nomad / Freakout, Taiwan Ska Fest at PIPE, Daniel Pearl World Music Day, Tiger Mountain Ramble, Taipei Veggie Fest, Celebration Canada, the European Christmas Market and other cultural events. There are recordings, original programmes and press links in the festival archive.

One special night was in May 2019, when Balkazar played as the backing band for Fermín Muguruza at Triangle in Taipei. He'd also performed in smaller projects including Earsore and BRIJ. And he still gets to play loud guitars with Fuzzy Wall.

Bars, clubs and one New Year's Eve on the beach.

There have been a lot of different stages along the way: Bobwundaye, Sappho, Revolver, James Joyce Irish Bar, Triangle, DAMN Bar, Red Room Rendezvous and more. One particularly memorable New Year's Eve was at Cijin Sunset Bar on the beach in Kaohsiung. The photo below is from that New Year's Eve show.

Sharing the music, not just playing gigs.

Marko has also introduced people to Balkan music through workshops. On September 20, 2020, he was one of the presenters at Balkan in Your Area, hosted by Being Music (存在音樂) in Taipei's Xinyi District. Together with Mark Spasić and Aleksandar Stmatov, the event introduced Balkan instruments, dance rhythms, improvisation and music from the region. He has also shared Balkan music at Vincent's Accordion Studio (蔡偉靖手風琴工作室).

What does he actually like playing? Old Balkan melodies on electric guitar, unusual rhythms like 7/8, and the freedom to add ska, reggae, rock or a bit of improvisation. Depending on the song, he might sing in Serbian, English, Spanish or Mandarin. It's still about having fun playing music with other people—and seeing whether the audience wants to dance.
